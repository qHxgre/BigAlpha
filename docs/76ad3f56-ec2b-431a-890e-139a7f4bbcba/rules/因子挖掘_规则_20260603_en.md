## Basic Rules

## Intellectual Property

* The intellectual property rights of competition entries (code, factor construction, reports, etc.) belong to the participating teams.
* The organizer holds **non-commercial rights to evaluation, display, and promotion** of all works, including but not limited to: publishing factor rankings and aggregated features through official channels, anonymously citing factor methods in post-competition reviews, and presenting in **desensitized form** at academic / industry exchanges.
* For award-winning outstanding factors, the organizer holds priority rights to negotiate commercial cooperation **under equivalent terms**; specific forms of commercial cooperation, consideration, and attribution will be subject to a separately signed written agreement, and these rules do not constitute commercial authorization themselves.
* For third-party resources used by participants (open-source libraries, pre-trained models, external APIs, etc.), licensing and ownership are the participant's own responsibility. See Section 5.2 for details.

### Fair Competition

Participants must abide by the following basic principles; specific identifiable violations are listed in Chapter 2 "Anti-Cheating and Violation Checklist".

* **Plagiarism** of others' code / factors (including past competitions, public repositories, or other teams) is strictly prohibited.
* **Cross-team sharing** of core code, factors, or model weights is strictly prohibited; **collusive score manipulation** (e.g., multiple teams submitting identical factors to push up their own relative rankings) is prohibited.
* It is strictly prohibited to **bypass, deceive, or attack** the evaluation system in any way (see Chapter 2 for details).
* Referring to public literature, open-source tools, and official template code is encouraged—work based on "reproduction + improvement" is welcome, but the **citation source and improvement points must be clearly indicated** in the final report.

### Track Compliance

* **Traditional Track**: It is strictly prohibited to use automated factor generation techniques exclusive to the AI track, including but not limited to:
  * Using Large Language Models (LLMs) to generate factor expressions in batches;
  * Using reinforcement learning / genetic algorithms / neural networks for automatic factor search or automatic combination;
  * Using AutoML tools for automated feature engineering of factors.
  * **Permitted**: Traditional statistical methods, manual factor construction driven by economic logic, and classical machine learning used for auxiliary analysis (e.g., using XGBoost for post-hoc attribution, but not as the main body of factor generation).
* **AI Track**: The core factor construction logic must be AI-driven; pseudo-AI (i.e., wrapping manual factors in an AI shell) is strictly prohibited. The final submission must additionally provide an **AI Application Description Document**, clearly indicating:
  * The type of AI technology used (LLM / RL / GA / NN, etc.);
  * The specific stages and code locations of AI in the factor generation / optimization pipeline;
  * Reproducible core information such as key prompts, training configurations, and loss functions;
  * Self-assessment of AI participation level (see the finals evaluation chapter for details).
* **Track Classification Disputes**: If the organizing committee has doubts about the track classification of a work, the team will be asked to supplement materials for clarification; if compliance still cannot be proven after clarification, it will be handled as a violation.

## Anti-Cheating and Violation Checklist

> In past competitions, some participants exploited gray areas in the competition design to improve their rankings. This chapter explicitly lists **identifiable violations**, and explains the detection mechanisms and handling gradients to avoid disputes after the fact.

### Data Usage Violations

* **Use of future data (look-ahead bias)**: Using post-close data on day t or data from day t+1 or later in factor calculations on day t. Including but not limited to: using day t closing prices to construct factor values for 09:45 on day t, or backfilling day t factors with financial report data from day t+5.
* **Unauthorized data access**: Using data outside the competition stock pool (CSI 1000 constituents at historical points in time), or using data outside the competition time range (2019-01-01 ~ 2024-12-31) to assist in constructing public-board factors.
* **Unauthorized upload of external data**: Uploading datasets not provided by the platform via Notebook (including but not limited to alternative data, overseas market data, third-party factor libraries) for participation in factor construction.
* **Data poisoning**: Manually modifying, falsifying, or injecting factor data files, rather than generating them automatically through code.

### Submission Evasion Violations

* **Using sparse factors to evade coverage validation**: Deliberately constructing factors with coverage just above 60% (i.e., 40% missing rate) but actually only effective on a small number of stocks, to evade validation.
* **Multi-factor splicing to bypass correlation constraints**: Artificially splitting one complete factor into multiple highly correlated sub-factors and submitting them separately, with the intent of occupying more ranking positions.
* **Rotating submissions to refresh rankings**: Repeatedly submitting the same factor with no substantive changes through minor adjustments, solely to trigger evaluation or refresh public-board display.
* **Reverse factor abuse**: When the factor direction is not significant, constructing a "new factor" merely by adding a negative sign.

### Identity and Collaboration Violations

* **Multi-account participation**: The same natural person registering multiple accounts and joining multiple teams.
* **Cross-team code / factor sharing**: Sharing factor code, model weights, or key prompts between different teams; including distribution under names such as "intra-mentorship division of labor" or "lab sharing".
* **Submission by proxy**: Others writing and submitting competition entries on behalf of someone, with that person making no substantive contribution to the work.
* **Collusive score manipulation**: Multiple teams conspiring to adjust submission strategies, with the intent of jointly raising one party's ranking or suppressing competitors.

### AI Track-Specific Violations

* **Pseudo-AI applications**: Packaging manually constructed factors under the guise of AI (e.g., using LLMs only for final "translation" or "beautification", while the factor remains substantively manually designed).
* **AI output forgery**: Fabricating AI invocation records, prompts, or training logs in the AI Application Description Document.
* **Misrepresentation of AI participation level**: The description of "AI participation level" in the finals stage being inconsistent with the actual code.

### Detection Mechanisms

The organizer will conduct violation detection through the following mechanisms; submission is deemed acceptance:

* **Static code analysis**: Scanning submitted code for suspicious patterns (e.g., time index shifts, external network requests, data exfiltration).
* **Factor correlation clustering**: Performing correlation matrix analysis on all factors entering the pool to identify abnormal clusters.
* **Cross-team fingerprint comparison**: Comparing structural similarity, variable naming, and comment style of code submitted by different teams.
* **Runtime auditing**: Sampling team code for reproduction in an isolated environment, and comparing whether the factor data is consistent with the team's claimed logic.
* **AI Track Specific Audit**: For AI track teams advancing to the finals, requiring reproduction of the core AI invocation pipeline.

## Data and Platform Disclaimer

* **Scope of Data Use**: Competition data is for **competition evaluation and learning research only**. It is strictly prohibited to disseminate externally, use commercially, upload to public repositories, or use for any scenario outside this competition, including after the competition ends. Once discovered, the organizer will pursue legal liability.
* **Data Confidentiality Period**: The confidentiality obligation for sensitive data such as high-frequency snapshots and PIT financial data provided by this competition is **permanently in effect**, and is not lifted upon the conclusion of the competition.
* **Data Accuracy**: The organizer has performed necessary cleaning and validation on the data, but does not guarantee absolute accuracy or completeness; fluctuations in factor performance caused by data issues are **generally not grounds for evaluation appeals**. However, severe data errors (e.g., bulk missing data, field misalignment, abnormal adjustments) will, after verification, trigger uniform recalculation, with synchronized explanation to all affected teams.
* **Platform Stability**: The BigQuant platform has been deployed with high availability, but may still experience short periods of unavailability due to force majeure (data center failure, network outages, third-party dependency anomalies, etc.). For submission delays caused by platform failures, the organizer will reasonably postpone deadlines or cancel evaluation results during the affected period based on the duration of the failure, but **will not provide economic compensation for participants' computational resource consumption or time investment**.
* **Compute Quota**: CPU Notebook ≤ 3 hours / session, AI track GPU Notebook ≤ 6 hours / session (see "Code Requirements" in the introduction document for details). Operational failures caused by exceeding the quota are the participant's own responsibility.
* **Results Do Not Constitute Investment Advice**: All factors, models, backtest results, and derivative analyses in this competition are for academic research and competition evaluation purposes only, and **do not constitute investment advice in any form**. The organizer, platform, and partners assume no liability for any profits or losses incurred by anyone conducting actual investments based on this.

* Participants are responsible for the code they submit, which must not contain:

  * Code attempting to bypass the platform sandbox, network isolation, or resource limits;

* Personal information provided by participants during registration is used only for competition-related purposes, and the organizer will protect it in accordance with the law, but participants must guarantee the authenticity and validity of the information provided.

* Submissions during the internal testing phase (2026-06-08 to 2026-06-18) do not count toward preliminary scores.
* Candidate factors after the preliminary deadline (no more than 2 per team, see introduction document for details) cannot be modified once selected; during the private board phase, participants **may not add or modify the construction code of any factor**.

* **Response Timeframes**:

| Appeal Type | Initial Response | Resolution |
|---|---|---|
| General appeal (evaluation results, disclosure information) | Within 5 business days | Within 10 business days |
| Violation handling objection (against L3 / L4) | Within 48 hours | Within 7 business days |
| Emergency appeal (involving deadlines, critical data) | Within 24 hours | Handled together with emergency revisions |

* **Repeat Appeals**: For repeat appeals on the same matter or appeals without new evidence, the organizing committee reserves the right to not respond separately.
