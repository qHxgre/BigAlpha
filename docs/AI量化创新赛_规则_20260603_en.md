> This document is the compliance and disclaimer document for the **BigAlpha 2026 Global University League · Open Innovation** track, used together with the companion introduction document of the same name. The "model" referred to in these rules specifically denotes the end-to-end deep learning model built by participants according to the "Model Specification" chapter of the introduction document, and the evaluation target is the cross-sectional score file output by that model.

## Basic Rules

### Intellectual Property

* The intellectual property of submitted works belongs to the participating teams.
* The organizer holds **non-commercial rights to evaluate, display, and promote** all works.
* For award-winning outstanding works, the organizer holds priority commercial cooperation negotiation rights **under equal conditions**; the specific form, consideration, and attribution of any commercial cooperation will be set out in a separately signed written agreement, and these rules do not in themselves constitute a commercial license.
* For third-party resources used by participants (open-source libraries, external APIs, etc.), the licensing and ownership are the participants' own responsibility — see "Participant Responsibilities · Third-Party Dependencies".

### Fair Competition

Participants must observe the following basic principles; specific identifiable violations are listed in Chapter 2 "Anti-Cheating and Violations Checklist".

* **Plagiarism** of others' code, model architectures, or weights (including from prior competitions, public repositories, or other teams) is strictly prohibited.
* **Cross-team sharing** of core code, model weights, or training hyperparameters is strictly prohibited; **score-rigging collusion is forbidden** (e.g., multiple teams submitting near-identical models to artificially inflate one team's relative ranking).
* Reference to public literature, open-source tools, and official template code is encouraged — work based on "reproduce + improve" is welcome, but the citation source and improvement points must be **clearly indicated** in the finals report.

### Identity and Collaboration Violations

* **Multi-account participation**: a single natural person registering multiple accounts or joining multiple teams.
* **Cross-team code / weight sharing**: different teams sharing training scripts, model weights, key prompts, or hyperparameters; including distribution under the guise of "intra-cohort division of labor" or "lab sharing".
* **Proxy submission**: another person writes and submits the work, with the registered participant making no substantive contribution.
* **Score-rigging collusion**: multiple teams conspiring to adjust submission strategies with the intent to jointly elevate one team's ranking or suppress competitors.

## Rule Versions and Changes

* **Rule version statement**: the current rules are an **initial draft** and may encounter unavoidable engineering or evaluation issues during the closed-beta phase (e.g., data interface changes, evaluation algorithm bottlenecks, third-party service anomalies). **The rule version published "after the closed-beta ends and before the formal competition begins" shall be authoritative**; participants should keep an eye on official announcements and community notices.
* **Right to adjust rules during the competition**: to ensure fairness, **the organizer reserves the right to revise the rules, adjust evaluation specifications, and add anti-cheating mechanisms during the competition**. Rule adjustments will take into account how already-submitted works are handled (e.g., re-scoring, retaining historical scores for reference, providing a reasonable code-adjustment window) so as to minimize impact on participants in good standing.
* **Change types and advance notice**:

| Change Type | Examples | Advance Notice |
|---|---|---|
| Major change | Scoring formula modification, submission spec adjustment, schedule postponement | ≥ 48 hours |
| General change | Public field adjustments, local tool version updates | ≥ 24 hours |
| Emergency revision | Anti-cheating patches, severe data / platform bug fixes, security vulnerability response | Immediate notice, with explanation provided afterwards |

* **Change notification channels**: official website announcement, official community (WeChat / QQ groups) pinned posts, and platform in-site messages — at least **two** of these three channels will be used in sync, with the in-site message delivery time treated as the formal effective time.
* **Historical version archive**: all rule versions will be retained on the official website with traceable history, allowing participants to verify the rules in force at the time of their submissions; version numbers use a "major.minor" format, with major changes incrementing the major version number.

## Data and Platform Disclaimer

* **Scope of data use**: competition data is provided **solely for competition evaluation and academic research**; it must not be redistributed, used commercially, uploaded to public repositories, or used in any scenario outside this competition, including after the competition ends. Violations will be pursued by the organizer in accordance with the law.
* **Data confidentiality term**: the confidentiality obligation for sensitive data such as high-frequency snapshots and PIT financial data provided in this competition is **of long-term effect** and is not lifted by the conclusion of the competition.
* **Data accuracy**: the organizer has performed necessary cleaning and validation on the data, but does not guarantee its absolute accuracy or completeness; model score fluctuations caused by data issues **are generally not grounds for evaluation appeals**, but verified severe data errors (e.g., bulk missing values, field misalignment, abnormal adjustment factors) will trigger a unified recomputation, and all affected teams will be notified.
* **Platform stability**: the BigQuant platform is deployed for high availability, but short outages may still occur due to force majeure (data center failures, network outages, third-party dependency anomalies, etc.). For submission delays caused by platform failures, the organizer will reasonably extend deadlines based on the duration of the failure or invalidate evaluation results from that period; **no economic compensation will be provided for participants' compute consumption or time investment**.
* **Compute quotas**: GPU Notebook single-shot inference / retraining ≤ 6 hours, with a 12-hour single-shot inference cap during the public-leaderboard phase (see "Code Requirements and Platform Constraints" in the introduction document). Run failures caused by exceeding the quota are the participant's responsibility; during the private-leaderboard retraining phase, the platform will enforce the above caps, and timeouts will be judged as a failed retrain of that model.
* **Results do not constitute investment advice**: all models, scores, backtest results, and derivative analyses produced in this competition are for academic research and competition evaluation only and **do not constitute investment advice of any kind**. The organizer, the platform, and partners bear no liability for any gains or losses arising from real-money investment based on these results.

## Participant Responsibilities

### Code Compliance and Security

* Participants are responsible for the code they submit, which must not contain:
  * Malicious code, backdoors, or logic that accesses platform resources without authorization;
  * Code that attempts to bypass the platform sandbox, network isolation, or resource limits;
  * Code that attempts to obtain other teams' submissions or intermediate evaluation results;
  * File operations that read from or write to non-sandbox paths in the private-leaderboard retraining environment.
* Once detected, the case will be handled at L4 immediately (disqualification), and the right to pursue legal liability is reserved.

### Third-Party Dependencies

* If participants use open-source libraries, external APIs, or other third-party resources, they must verify on their own that the licenses **permit use in competition and commercial-evaluation scenarios**.
* **Use of any external pre-trained weights is forbidden** — including but not limited to public HuggingFace checkpoints, torchvision pre-trained models, and transfer-learning weights from the participant's own projects. All model parameters must be trained from scratch on the data provided in this competition.
* Copyright / licensing disputes arising from third-party dependencies are borne by the participants themselves; the organizer reserves the right to require teams to replace disputed dependencies.

### Personal Information and Likeness

* Personal information provided by participants at registration is used solely for competition-related purposes; the organizer will protect such information in accordance with the law, and participants must ensure the information provided is true and valid.
* Teams advancing to the finals consent to the organizer's use of their **names, schools, team names, and finals footage** in competition publicity; if a team wishes to remain anonymous, it must submit a written request when confirming finals advancement.

### Closed-Beta and Freeze-Period Constraints

* Submissions during the closed-beta phase (2026-06-08 to 2026-06-18) do not count toward preliminary scores, and the leaderboard will be reset after the closed-beta ends.
* Before the public-leaderboard deadline, each team must **designate exactly one end-to-end model** from its submission history as the team's private-leaderboard model (see "Submission Spec and Frequency" in the introduction document); if no designation is made by the deadline, the platform will default to the team's highest-scoring public-leaderboard submission.
* Once a candidate model is designated, it is frozen — during the private-leaderboard phase, participants **may not add to or modify its training script, inference script, dependency manifest, hyperparameter configuration, or random seed**; the submitted code package must be able to independently complete training and inference within the platform's isolated environment.

## Appeals and Final Interpretation

### Appeals Process

* **Appeal scope**: disputes over evaluation results (including public-leaderboard score anomalies, private-leaderboard retraining failures, abnormal style-neutralization residuals, etc.), objections to violation handling, severe data errors, etc.
* **Appeal channels**: contact the competition operations staff and attach the necessary evidence (screenshots, logs, code snippets, etc.). Appeals via **non-official channels** (privately contacting judges, flooding community groups, etc.) will not be accepted.
* **Response timelines**:

| Appeal Type | Initial Response | Resolution |
|---|---|---|
| General appeal (evaluation results, public information) | within 5 business days | within 10 business days |
| Objection to violation handling | within 48 hours | within 7 business days |
| Urgent appeal (involving deadlines, critical data) | within 24 hours | handled together with the emergency revision |

* **Repeated appeals**: the organizing committee reserves the right to not respond separately to repeated appeals on the same matter or appeals without new evidence.

### Final Interpretation

The right of final interpretation of all rules in this competition belongs to the **BigAlpha 2026 Competition Organizing Committee**. In case of any ambiguity between the Chinese version of these rules and any translation, the **Chinese version shall prevail**.
