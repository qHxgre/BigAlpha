### Why Factor Mining

In modern quantitative investment, alpha factors are the core of generating excess returns. An effective factor can accurately predict future stock price movements from massive, noisy market data. Continuously mining and iterating high-quality factors is key to driving innovation in the quantitative investment industry.

We believe the most outstanding investment ideas often come from open exploration and collaboration. Therefore, we chose to open up real, high-granularity market data through this track and invite top university talent worldwide to take on this challenge.

Participants will gain access to scarce, high-quality minute-level market data of the A-share market, with the task of using this data to build innovative factors that predict future stock returns. Your factors will be evaluated in two stages: first backtested on historical data to obtain a "Public Leaderboard" score, then simulated on fresh, dynamically updated market data to obtain a "Private Leaderboard" score, ensuring strategy robustness in the real world.

Through this track, you will gain:

* Hands-on experience processing large-scale financial time-series data;
* In-depth understanding of market microstructure;
* Direct exposure to real quantitative challenges—how to adapt different technical approaches (traditional methods / AI techniques) to drive factor mining, and how to maintain robustness across high, medium, and low frequency strategy scenarios;
* The opportunity to compete with top university talent worldwide and have your work seen by institutions and sponsors.

## Data Validation and Preprocessing

Submitted factor data must pass all the following validations, otherwise the submission will be considered invalid.

* **Column Check**: The submission file must contain exactly three columns — `date` (trading day), `instrument` (stock code), `factor` (factor value).
    * The platform does not restrict factor direction. By default, **larger factor values are better**. Participants must ensure the logical correctness of factor direction themselves.
* **Trading Day Completeness**: Factor data must not miss any trading day within the evaluation time range.
* **Factor Coverage**: On each trading day, the missing rate of factor values must not exceed **40%**.
* **Preprocessing**: Apply **outlier removal** and **standardization** to the raw factor.
* **Style Removal**: Regress the raw factor against BARRA risk factors and take the residual as the new factor to evaluate the factor's incremental contribution.

## Factor Pool and Evaluation

### Submission Rules

Each team can submit a maximum of **50 factors** and select **n of them** ($1 \le n \le 50$) to enter the team's factor pool for evaluation.

> Design intent of the submission cap: encourage participants to curate factors rather than stack them in bulk; also reserves reasonable computational resources for platform evaluation.

### Single-Factor Score (Item A)

Calculate the single-factor score independently for each factor entered into the pool:

$$
\text{FACTOR} \;=\; 0.25 \times \text{Rank}_{IC_{mean}} \;+\; 0.25 \times \text{Rank}_{IC_{IR}} \;+\; 0.25 \times \text{Rank}_{SR} \;+\; 0.25 \times \text{Rank}_{\text{Stress}}
$$

* $\text{Rank}_{IC_{mean}}$: IC mean ranking.
* $\text{Rank}_{IC_{IR}}$: IC_IR ranking.
* $\text{Rank}_{SR}$: long-short portfolio Sharpe ratio ranking.
* $\text{Rank}_{\text{Stress}}$: IC_IR score under stressed market conditions.

**Team Item A Score**: Take the highest single-factor score among all factors entered by the team:

$$
\text{Score}_A^{(\text{team})} = \max_{i \in \text{team}} \text{FACTOR}_i
$$

### Elastic Net Regression Score (Item B)

Aggregate the factor pools of all participating teams into a global candidate factor set. With the cross-sectional z-score standardized next-period return as the target, perform Elastic Net regression:

$$
y_{i,t} = \frac{r_{i,t+1} - \mu_t}{\sigma_t}, \quad r = Fw + \epsilon
$$

$$
\mathcal{L} = \|y - Fw\|^2 + \lambda_1 \|w\|_1 + \lambda_2 \|w\|^2
$$

Use a **rolling window** for training (window length 60 trading days, step size 20 trading days). For each factor, take the cross-window weight stability score:

$$
\text{ModelScore}_i = \frac{\text{mean}(|w_i|)}{\text{std}(|w_i|) + \epsilon}
$$

> This metric simultaneously measures the average contribution of the factor in the portfolio and its cross-period stability, analogous to IC_IR in single-factor evaluation. The L1 term of Elastic Net pushes the weights of factors with no incremental contribution to 0, while the L2 term smooths the weight allocation among correlated factor groups, avoiding random selection.

**Team Item B Score**: Take the mean ModelScore of all factors selected (with non-zero weight) by Elastic Net for the team:

$$
\text{Score}_B^{(\text{team})} = \text{mean}_{i \in \mathcal{S}_{\text{team}}} \widetilde{\text{ModelScore}}_i, \quad \mathcal{S}_{\text{team}} = \{i \in \text{team} \mid w_i > 0\}
$$

where $\widetilde{\text{ModelScore}}_i$ is the percentile-normalized ModelScore, and $\mathcal{S}_{\text{team}}$ is the set of factors of the team with non-zero weights. If no factor of the team is selected, then $\text{Score}_B = 0$.

> Whether a factor is selected is entirely determined by the L1 term of Elastic Net—no additional manual parameters are needed. The mean rewards "average quality"—submitting more factors with no incremental contribution will lower the score, naturally preventing winning by quantity.

### Local Debugging Support

To facilitate participants in evaluating factor quality before submission, the platform provides a **local regression tool**:

* Participants can input their own factor library and locally run the same Elastic Net regression process as the official one.
* The tool outputs each factor's estimated ModelScore, rolling weight curve, and correlation heatmap with other factors.
* The local tool uses a **public historical factor set** (the base factor library provided by the organizers) as a control group to simulate the global competitive environment.

> Local results are for reference only. The final score is based on the official global regression (since the global factor set changes with the competition progress).

## Evaluation Cycle and Ranking

This competition is divided into a **Public Leaderboard Stage** and a **Private Leaderboard Stage**, using different data ranges. The final ranking is based on the Private Leaderboard.

### Data Split

| Stage | Training Set | Validation Set | Description |
|---|---|---|---|
| Public | 2019-01-01 ~ 2024-12-31 | Full year of 2025 | Validation set scores visible to participants for debugging and iteration |
| Private | Not disclosed | Not disclosed | Includes 2026 out-of-sample data; specific range is not publicly disclosed |

> The public leaderboard validation set (2025) does not overlap with the private leaderboard evaluation range, preventing participants from indirectly fitting the private leaderboard data through repeated submissions.

### Evaluation Cycle

In the public leaderboard stage, every new factor submission joins the queue to be evaluated. However, since the computational cost of Elastic Net grows with the global factor count N, to avoid resource waste, the platform uses an **adaptive interval**:

$$t_{\text{next}} = \max\bigl(k \cdot t_{\text{last\_run}},\; t_{\text{min}}\bigr)$$

* $t_{\text{last\_run}}$: actual evaluation time of the previous round.
* $k$: safety factor, set to 1.5, leaving margin for the next round of factor increments.
* $t_{\text{min}}$: minimum interval, fixed at **1 hour**, avoiding overly frequent triggers.

In the early competition phase, factors are few and computation is fast, so the interval is naturally short; in the later phase, the factor pool expands, and the interval automatically lengthens, requiring no manual intervention.

After each round of evaluation, the platform synchronously updates the public leaderboard ranking and publishes the round's evaluation results.

**Private Leaderboard Stage**: After the public leaderboard ends, participants' factors are frozen, and no additions or modifications are allowed. The platform performs a one-time evaluation of all frozen factors using the private leaderboard data, and the result is the final ranking.

### Team Final Score

The public leaderboard score is for reference only and does not count toward the final ranking. After the private leaderboard stage ends, the final score is calculated as follows:

$$
\text{Score}_{\text{final}} = 0.3 \times \text{Score}_A^{(\text{team})} + 0.7 \times \text{Score}_B^{(\text{team})}
$$

Teams are ranked in descending order of $\text{Score}_{\text{final}}$ to obtain the final team ranking. The two tracks are ranked independently.

> Disclosing competitive information increases competitive intensity while letting participants clearly see the gap between themselves and the leading teams.

### Index Enhancement Strategy Tracking

Based on the factor weights obtained from each round of regression, the platform synchronously builds a **CSI 1000 Index Enhancement Strategy**:

* Using the synthetic factor value from Elastic Net regression as the signal, overweight high-scoring stocks and underweight low-scoring stocks within the constituents, with tracking error constrained to within 5%.
* After each round of evaluation, the strategy holdings are updated and the following metrics are displayed:

| Metric | Description |
|---|---|
| Cumulative Excess Return | Cumulative alpha relative to CSI 1000 |
| Annualized Excess Return / Tracking Error | Information Ratio (IR) |
| Maximum Drawdown | Maximum drawdown of excess return |
| Incremental Contribution After This Round of Factor Updates | Change in strategy IR after the new round of factors are added |

> The continuous improvement of the index-enhanced strategy is an intuitive reflection of competition quality—new high-quality factors added in each round should drive the strategy IR up. If a round's new factors fail to improve strategy performance, this will also be honestly presented in the disclosure, providing positive feedback to participants.
