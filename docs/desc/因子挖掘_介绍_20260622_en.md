## Competition Overview

**BigAlpha** is BigQuant's (Broad Tech) annual AI quantitative investment research competition for young talent at universities worldwide. Built on the BigQuant AI investment research platform, it discovers the next generation of fintech talent through real data, open problems, and professional review.

BigAlpha 2026 opens three competitions globally and simultaneously. Participants are free to choose:

| Track | Suited For | Link |
|---|---|---|
| **AI Factor Mining** | Financial engineering, quant, math, statistics | [Enter](https://bigquant.com/square/competition/76ad3f56-ec2b-431a-890e-139a7f4bbcba) |
| **End-to-End Price-Volume Time-Series Forecasting** | AI, CS, data science, fintech | [Enter](https://bigquant.com/square/competition/523f9302-5b4b-42bd-bce1-f232e7c74316) |
| **AI Open Innovation** | Interdisciplinary, innovation & entrepreneurship teams | [Enter](https://bigquant.com/square/competition/63dd885c-2488-4efd-9c61-9e3a536f172c) |

**About BigQuant**: A leading domestic AI quantitative investment platform whose mission is to "Democratize AI to empower investors." The platform deeply integrates AI technologies such as machine learning and deep learning with quantitative investment, providing individual quants, research institutions, brokerages, and asset managers with a one-stop solution spanning data, research, backtesting, and live trading. It has been adopted by many leading financial institutions. Core capabilities include petabyte-scale financial and alternative data, a library of 2,000+ base factors with an expression engine, a high-performance DAI data engine, and a strategy source-code library with live-trading integration. This track uses the very same toolchain as institutional clients.

**Why factor mining**: Alpha factors are the core of how quantitative investing captures excess returns — predicting future stock price movements from noisy market data. This track opens scarce, high-granularity minute-level A-share market data and invites university talent worldwide to build innovative factors. Factors are evaluated in two stages: first, a **public leaderboard** score from backtesting on historical data, then a **private leaderboard** score on dynamically updated out-of-sample data to test robustness in real conditions. Participants gain hands-on experience with large-scale financial time-series data, an understanding of market microstructure, and the opportunity for their work to be seen by institutions and sponsors.

## Track Setup

The competition runs two parallel tracks. Participants build and submit factors according to their chosen track, and the two tracks are ranked independently.

* **Traditional Quant Track**
  * **Core**: Emphasizes investment logic, statistical significance, and economic interpretability.
  * **Methods**: Multi-factor models, fundamental analysis, classic technical-indicator combinations, physics-model simulations, etc.
  * **Review focus**: Soundness of economic logic, significance of statistical tests, robustness across market regimes, factor interpretability.

* **AI Intelligence Track**
  * **Core**: Uses techniques such as large language models (LLMs), reinforcement learning, and genetic algorithms to automatically generate or optimize factors.
  * **Methods**: Automated feature engineering, prompt engineering to generate factor formulas, neural networks to mine nonlinear factors, etc.
  * **Review focus**: Beyond factor effectiveness, additionally evaluates the depth, innovativeness, and degree of AI involvement (see Finals Evaluation).

## Data

* **Stock universe**: Constituents of the CSI 1000 Index at the corresponding historical points in time.
* **Time range**: 2019-01-01 to 2024-12-31.
* **Data contents**:
  * **K-line and order-book snapshots**: 1-minute K-line and order-book data.
  * **Financial data**: Processed as PIT (Point-in-Time).

## Participation & Submission

### Template Code

This track follows a "factor mining" approach: the platform provides distinctive data such as high-frequency data, and participants build and submit **daily-frequency factors**. The organizers provide the following templates for reference:

| Template | Description |
|---|---|
| `factor_financial.ipynb` | ROE_TTM = Net Profit Attributable to Parent TTM / Net Assets Attributable to Parent LF |
| `factor_hf.ipynb` | Computes intraday order book pressure from 1-minute order book snapshots, aggregated to daily frequency by trading day |
| `factor_model.ipynb` | Features combining price-volume factors and financial factors, trained for prediction using XGBoost |

> Note: You may submit multiple files of different types, but ensure that the code calling the evaluation module is placed within the notebook. Only one notebook should be included in the submission; the backend will extract and run the code from it.

### Submission Format

Simply write your factor-construction code in a **main** function and submit it. The platform automatically runs it to generate factor data, computes the score, and publishes it in real time. What you submit must be **code** that runs automatically, not the factor data file itself. The data returned by `main` must contain exactly three columns and no others:

| date | instrument | factor |
|---|---|---|
| 2023-01-03 | 000001.SZ | 0.05 |
| 2023-01-03 | 000002.SZ | -0.12 |
| ... | ... | ... |

* The platform places no restriction on factor direction; by default a **larger factor value is better**, and participants must ensure the direction logic is correct themselves.
* Traditional-track code should annotate where the core statistical methods and economic logic are implemented; AI-track code should annotate the key points where AI techniques are applied (LLM calls, model training, automated feature engineering, etc.) and additionally submit a document describing the AI techniques used.
* Text-based information (such as prompts when AI is used) should be submitted in markdown format.

### Data Validation

Submitted factors must pass all of the following checks, or they are deemed invalid:

* **Data columns**: Must contain exactly the three columns `date`, `instrument`, and `factor`.
* **Trading-day completeness**: No trading day within the evaluation period may be missing.
* **Factor coverage**: For each trading day, the factor's missing-value rate must not exceed **40%**.

### Platform Preprocessing

Once validation passes, the platform applies uniform processing to the raw factor:

* **Winsorization** and **standardization**.
* **Style neutralization**: Regress the factor on BARRA risk factors and take the residual as the new factor, to evaluate its incremental contribution.

### Runtime Environment Requirements

To ensure fairness, all factor-generation code must run in and be submitted from the designated online platform Notebook environment:

* **Runtime limit**: Notebook ≤ 3 hours.
* **No external network**: To prevent information leakage and the use of future data, internet access from the Notebook is disabled.

## Scoring Mechanism

### Submission Rules & Factor Pool

Each team may submit at most **50 factors**, with each submission corresponding to one factor. All of a team's valid submissions form its factor pool — no additional selection is required.

> Intent of the cap: encourage curated factors rather than bulk stacking, while reserving reasonable compute for platform evaluation.

Scoring uses the **single submission (a single factor) as the smallest scoring unit**: each submission's composite score is computed independently, and a team's leaderboard score is the maximum across all of the team's submissions.

$$
\text{Score}^{(\text{team})} = \max_{i \in \text{team}} \text{Score}_i, \qquad \text{Score}_i = 0.3 \times A_i + 0.7 \times B_i
$$

where $A_i$ and $B_i$ are both scores of **factor $i$ itself**.

### Term A: Single-Factor Score

Computed independently for each factor:

$$
A_i = 0.25 \times \text{Rank}_{IC_{mean}} + 0.25 \times \text{Rank}_{IC_{IR}} + 0.25 \times \text{Rank}_{SR} + 0.25 \times \text{Rank}_{\text{Stress}}
$$

* $\text{Rank}_{IC_{mean}}$: Rank of the IC mean.
* $\text{Rank}_{IC_{IR}}$: Rank of IC_IR.
* $\text{Rank}_{SR}$: Rank of the long-short portfolio Sharpe ratio.
* $\text{Rank}_{\text{Stress}}$: IC_IR score under special market regimes.

All four are the result of cross-sectional percentile ranking (pct rank) across **all submitted factors**, taking values in $[0, 1]$.

### Term B: Elastic Net Regression Score

All teams' submitted factors are aggregated into a global candidate set, and an Elastic Net regression is run against the cross-sectionally z-score-standardized next-period return as the target:

$$
y_{i,t} = \frac{r_{i,t+1} - \mu_t}{\sigma_t}, \quad r = Fw + \epsilon
$$

$$
\mathcal{L} = \|y - Fw\|^2 + \lambda_1 \|w\|_1 + \lambda_2 \|w\|^2
$$

Training uses a **rolling window** (window of 60 trading days, step of 20 trading days), and for each factor a cross-window weight-stability score is taken:

$$
\text{ModelScore}_i = \frac{\text{mean}(|w_i|)}{\text{std}(|w_i|) + \epsilon}
$$

> This metric measures both a factor's average contribution to the portfolio and its cross-period stability, analogous to IC_IR in single-factor evaluation. The L1 term shrinks factors with no incremental contribution to zero weight, and the L2 term smoothly distributes weights within a group of correlated factors.

A factor's Term B score is the percentile of its ModelScore across all regressed factors, falling in $[0, 1]$:

$$
B_i = \widetilde{\text{ModelScore}}_i
$$

If a factor is not selected by Elastic Net (its weight is identically 0), then $B_i = 0$.

> Whether a factor is selected is determined entirely by the L1 term, with no manual parameters. Factors with no incremental contribution are naturally shrunk to a score of 0, so stacking low-quality factors yields no benefit — naturally preventing winning by quantity.

### Team Final Score

$$
\text{Score}_{\text{final}} = \max_{i \in \text{team}} \left( 0.3 \times A_i + 0.7 \times B_i \right)
$$

### Local Debugging Support

The platform provides a **local regression tool** so you can assess factor quality yourself before submitting:

* Input your own factor library and run, locally, the same Elastic Net process as the official one.
* Outputs each factor's estimated ModelScore, rolling weight curves, and a correlation heatmap with other factors.
* Uses a **public historical factor set** (the organizers' base factor library) as a benchmark to simulate the global competitive environment.

> Local results are for reference only; the final score is determined by the official global regression (the global factor set changes as the competition progresses).

## Evaluation Cycle & Ranking

The competition has two stages, the **public leaderboard** and the **private leaderboard**, using different data ranges; the final ranking is based on the private leaderboard.

### Data Split

| Stage | Training Set | Validation Set | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Public | 2019-01-01 ~ 2024-12-31 | All of 2025 | Validation scores are visible, used for debugging and iteration |
| Private | Not disclosed | Not disclosed | Includes 2026 out-of-sample data; range not disclosed |

> The public validation set (2025) does not overlap the private range, preventing indirect fitting of the private leaderboard through repeated submissions.

### Evaluation Interval (Public Stage)

Each new submission joins the evaluation queue. Because the Elastic Net computation grows with the number of global factors, the platform uses an **adaptive interval**:

$$t_{\text{next}} = \max\bigl(k \cdot t_{\text{last\_run}},\; t_{\text{min}}\bigr)$$

* $t_{\text{last\_run}}$: the actual time the last round of evaluation took; $k = 1.5$ is a safety factor; $t_{\text{min}} = 1$ hour is the minimum interval.

Early in the competition there are few factors and the interval is short; later the factor pool grows and the interval lengthens automatically, with no manual intervention. After each round of evaluation, the public leaderboard ranking is updated accordingly.

**Private stage**: After the public leaderboard closes, factors are frozen and may not be added or modified. During the private range, the platform incrementally builds factors and computes scores after each trading day's close; the final result is the final ranking.

### Disclosures Each Round

At a fixed time each day, the following information is published based on the latest evaluation results, helping participants clarify directions for optimization.

**(1) Top-10 factor profiles** (top 10 by ModelScore)

| Feature | Description |
|---|---|
| BARRA style exposure | Exposure distribution across styles such as size, beta, momentum, and volatility, hinting at dimensions not yet fully explored |
| Industry distribution | Average IC per industry, revealing the factor's industry preference |

> What is published are **aggregate features**, not specific construction logic, so it does not affect intellectual-property protection.

**(2) Team detail scores**

* Of the current top-20 factors by weight, how many belong to your team (without disclosing other teams' factor contents).
* The ModelScore percentile rank and its trend over time for each of your team's factors.
* Your team's Term A and Term B scores and your percentile rank across the field.

**(3) Index-enhancement strategy tracking**

Based on each round's regression weights, the platform builds a **CSI 1000 index-enhancement strategy**: using the synthetic factor value as the signal, it overweights high-scoring stocks and underweights low-scoring stocks within the constituents, subject to a tracking-error constraint of about 5%. Each round it updates holdings and displays:

| Metric | Description |
|---|---|
| Cumulative excess return | Cumulative alpha relative to CSI 1000 |
| Information ratio (IR) | Annualized excess return / tracking error |
| Maximum drawdown | Maximum drawdown of the excess return |
| Incremental contribution | Change in strategy IR after the new round's factors are added |

> Continuous improvement of the index-enhancement strategy is a direct reflection of competition quality — high-quality new factors should push IR up; if they fail to improve it, that is also shown faithfully, creating positive feedback.

## Finals

### Submission Materials

Finalist teams must submit a factor research report on time (PDF, 10–15 pages), structured to include:

| Section | Key Content |
|---|---|
| Abstract | Research objective, core method, and main conclusions |
| Track label | Clearly state "Traditional" or "AI" track |
| Introduction | Research background and literature review |
| Factor construction | Data preprocessing, factor formulas and logic (AI track additionally describes where AI is applied) |
| Empirical analysis | Backtest results, performance attribution, robustness tests (across periods and industries) |
| Innovation & limitations | Methodological innovations and limitations; AI track describes AI-application innovations |
| Conclusion | Research summary and future directions |

### Defense & Awards

The finals defense follows a **"25-minute presentation + 5-minute Q&A with judges"** format, held offline with simultaneous live streaming online. After the defense, final rankings are announced and an awards ceremony is held.

### Evaluation Criteria

The review committee evaluates comprehensively:

* **Factor quality & logic**: Investment logic and economic interpretability; effectiveness and robustness across periods and industries; statistical significance.
* **Research depth & rigor**: Rigor of report structure and depth of analysis; code standards, readability, and reproducibility.
* **Live performance**: Clarity and organization of the presentation; precision and logic in the Q&A.
* **AI innovation** (AI track): Appropriateness of AI technology selection and scenario fit, originality of the application, and the practical effect of the degree of AI involvement.
