### Why End-to-End Models

In the evolution of quantitative investment, the traditional two-step paradigm of "factor mining + portfolio optimization" is facing dual challenges of information loss and human bias. Traditional methods rely heavily on manual experience to construct features, often making it difficult to capture rapidly changing nonlinear, high-order interaction relationships in the market. Therefore, **End-to-End quantitative modeling** has emerged. Its core idea is to use deep neural networks to skip cumbersome explicit feature engineering, directly learning investment logic from raw market sequence data, achieving a one-stop mapping from "raw data" to "investment decisions".

In this competition, we focus on **end-to-end model generation**. Participants will no longer be required to mine isolated factors, but instead need to build a large model capable of handling complex spatio-temporal relationships. Using multi-frequency minute-level market data provided by the organizer as input, the training target is the residual return of stocks. We encourage participants to adopt feature-free or weak-feature R&D modes, using raw price-volume sequences (such as OHLCV, intraday snapshots) as input as much as possible, leveraging the model's internal mechanisms to spontaneously capture the deep patterns of market microstructure.

## Model Specifications

### Input and Feature Constraints

1. **Feature Limit**: Model input fields shall not exceed **100 raw fields**. The same field at different time steps in a time-series window still counts as 1 field (i.e., time-series length does not count toward the field count).
2. **Data Source**: All input fields must come directly from the data provided by the organizer. **Any derived or manual feature engineering is prohibited**, including cross-field operators, rolling statistics, factor synthesis, dimensionality reduction, third-party data, etc.
3. **Allowed Preprocessing**: Missing value imputation, field-uniform normalization (MinMax / StandardScaler), field-uniform logarithmic or sign transformations. Preprocessing parameters may only be derived from training set statistics.
4. **Time-Series Window Limit**: Single inference look-back window $\le$ **240 trading days**.

### Model Architecture Constraints

1. **Trainable Parameter Count**: $\ge$ 100,000 (100K), $\le$ 100,000,000 (100M).
2. **External Pre-trained Weights**: The use of any external pre-trained weights is prohibited. All parameters must be trained from scratch by participants based on this competition's data.
3. **Architecture Form**: Not strictly specified, but the combination of the parameter count lower bound and the prohibition on feature engineering means that shallow models (XGBoost, linear models) will struggle to achieve competitive results.

### Model Output Format

Each submission's model inference produces a score file that must contain exactly three columns (by default, higher scores are better; participants must ensure the sign is correct):

| date | instrument | score |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2023-01-03 09:45:00 | 000001.SZ | 0.05 |
| 2023-01-03 09:45:00 | 000002.SZ | -0.12 |

## Evaluation System

The scores produced, after style elimination, are equivalent to a single factor updated daily. Therefore, the single-factor score is directly used as the team score.

### Data Validation

Submitted score files must pass the following validation, otherwise they are considered invalid submissions:

* **Data Column Check**: Column names strictly match `date / instrument / score`, with no extra columns.
* **Trading Day Completeness**: No trading days within the evaluation period may be missing.
* **Cross-Sectional Coverage**: For each trading day, the score missing rate must not exceed **40%**.

### Platform Preprocessing Pipeline

After receiving the submitted scores, the platform will perform the following operations in sequence:

1. **Outlier Removal**: Cross-sectional 1% / 99% quantile winsorization.
2. **Standardization**: Cross-sectional z-score.
3. **Style Elimination**: Cross-sectional regression of standardized scores against BARRA risk factors (market cap, Beta, momentum, volatility, liquidity, valuation, profitability, growth, leverage, non-linear market cap), **taking the residuals as the final evaluation target**.

### Final Scoring Formula

$$\text{Score}_{\text{final}} \;=\; 0.25 \times \text{Rank}_{IC_{mean}} \;+\; 0.25 \times \text{Rank}_{IC_{IR}} \;+\; 0.25 \times \text{Rank}_{SR} \;+\; 0.25 \times \text{Rank}_{\text{Stress}}$$

* $\text{Rank}_{IC_{mean}}$: Overall ranking of cross-sectional IC mean within the evaluation period.
* $\text{Rank}_{IC_{IR}}$: Ranking of IR (IC mean / IC standard deviation) of the IC sequence.
* $\text{Rank}_{SR}$: Ranking of annualized Sharpe ratio of the long-short 10-decile portfolio constructed using model scores.
* $\text{Rank}_{\text{Stress}}$: Ranking of robustness evaluated by regime.

> All four items are normalized to percentiles and added with equal weights. The team's final ranking is determined by sorting $\text{Score}_{\text{final}}$ in descending order.

### Submission Specifications and Frequency

Public board phase:

* **Each submission must be a complete model package**, including training scripts, inference scripts, dependency declarations, hyperparameter configurations, random seeds, and **trained weights** obtained on the public-board training set. During the public board phase, the platform **only performs inference based on submitted weights**, without retraining, thereby saving participants from the repetitive training cost caused by platform retraining.
* Each team can submit a maximum of **3 times** per day (including failed submissions).
* Submissions **do not overwrite each other**: each submission is recorded as an independent entry, separately inferred and scored on the public-board interval, with its score displayed. Participants may simultaneously hold and iterate multiple candidate models, without needing to pre-select a particular set of weights during iteration.
* Before the public-board deadline, each team must **designate a single end-to-end model** from their historical submissions as the private-board competition model. Teams that fail to designate by the deadline will have the platform default to their highest-scoring public-board submission. Once designation is confirmed, it is frozen, and participants may not add or modify its code, configuration, or dependencies.

After entering the private board phase:

1. **Platform Retraining**: In an isolated environment, the platform **retrains the unique model from scratch** using a specific training set according to the training scripts and configurations submitted by participants. The public-board weights submitted by participants do not participate in private-board evaluation.
2. **Private Board Inference**: The retrained weights are used to perform inference and scoring on the private-board validation interval.

## Per-Round Feedback Mechanism

At a fixed time daily during the public board phase, based on the latest evaluation results, the platform publishes the following information to help participants clarify optimization directions:

### Model Profile (Public Aggregated Features of Top 10)

For the **submissions** ranked top 10 in the current public-board total score (deduplicated by team, only the highest-scoring submission per team is taken), the following **aggregated** features are published (not involving specific network structures):

| Feature | Description |
|---|---|
| BARRA Style Exposure | Distribution of pre-style-elimination model scores across styles such as market cap, Beta, momentum, volatility |
| Industry IC Distribution | Average IC of the model across each Shenwan first-level industry, revealing industry preferences |
| Cross-Sectional Dispersion | Time-series mean of cross-sectional std of scores, measuring whether predictions are overly concentrated |

> What is published are aggregated features, not specific model structures, and does not affect intellectual property protection.

### Team Detailed Scores

After each round of evaluation, the following are privately pushed to each team:

* The team's total score for **every valid submission**, the overall percentile of the four sub-scores (IC mean / IC_IR / SR / Stress), and the team's current highest-scoring submission.
* Decomposition of the gap between the current highest-scoring submission and the 1st place and 10th place in each sub-score, locating the weakest item.
* Score trajectory of the past 30 evaluation days (aggregated by submission, with options to switch to view the trajectories of different submissions of this team).

### Index Enhancement Strategy Tracking

Based on the score of each team's **current highest-scoring public-board submission**, the platform synchronously builds a **CSI 1000 Index Enhancement Strategy**:

* Using model scores as signals, overweight high-scoring stocks and underweight low-scoring stocks within constituents, with tracking error constrained to within 5%.
* Holdings are updated after each round of evaluation, displaying:

| Metric | Description |
|---|---|
| Cumulative Excess Return | Cumulative alpha relative to CSI 1000 |
| Annualized Excess / Tracking Error | Information Ratio (IR) |
| Maximum Drawdown | Maximum drawdown of excess returns |
| Incremental Contribution After Model Iteration | Change in strategy IR after a new round of submissions |

> If the end-to-end model truly extracts incremental information from raw data, it should continuously drive the IR of the index enhancement strategy upward; if a certain round of iteration fails to improve strategy performance, this will also be honestly presented as feedback to participants.
