> This document is the compliance and disclaimer document for the **BigAlpha 2026 Global University League · End-to-End Model** track, used together with the introduction document of the same name. The "model" referred to in these rules specifically denotes the end-to-end deep learning model built by participants according to the "Model Specification" chapter of the introduction document. The evaluation target is the cross-sectional score file produced by this model.

## Basic Rules

### Intellectual Property

* The intellectual property of submitted works (including training scripts, inference scripts, model weights, configurations, and documentation) belongs to the participating teams.
* The organizer holds **non-commercial rights for evaluation, display, and promotion** of all submissions.
* For award-winning outstanding works, the organizer holds priority rights to negotiate commercial cooperation **under equivalent terms**; the specific form, consideration, and attribution of any commercial cooperation will be governed by a separate written agreement. These rules do not constitute a commercial license themselves.
* For third-party resources used by participants (open-source libraries, external APIs, etc.), the licensing and ownership are the participants' responsibility, as detailed in "Participant Responsibilities · Third-Party Dependencies." This track **prohibits the use of any external pre-trained weights** (see "Model Architecture Constraints" in the introduction document). Compliance and ownership disputes arising from this are borne by the participants.

### Fair Competition

Participants must adhere to the following basic principles. Specific identifiable violations are listed in Chapter 2 "Anti-Cheating and Violation Checklist."

* **Plagiarism** of others' code, model architecture, or weights (including from past competitions, public repositories, or other teams) is strictly prohibited.
* **Cross-team sharing** of core code, model weights, or training hyperparameters is strictly prohibited; **collusion to inflate scores** (e.g., multiple teams cross-submitting similar models to boost their relative rankings) is forbidden.
* Any attempt to **bypass, deceive, or attack** the evaluation system is strictly prohibited (see Chapter 2).
* Reference to public literature, open-source tools, and official template code is encouraged—work based on "reproduction + improvement" is welcomed, but the **citation source and improvement points must be clearly marked** in the final report.

## Anti-Cheating and Violation Checklist

> In past competitions, some participants exploited gray areas in the contest design to improve rankings. This chapter explicitly enumerates **identifiable violations**, along with the detection mechanisms and handling tiers, to avoid post-hoc disputes.

### Data Usage Violations

* **Using future data (look-ahead bias)**: Using data from after market close on day t or from day t+1 or later in inference for day t. Includes but is not limited to: using day t closing price to construct input features for 09:45 of day t, backfilling day t features with day t+5 financial reports, mixing future-time-step statistics into cross-sample batch normalization.
* **Unauthorized data access**: Using data outside the contest's stock universe (CSI 1000 historical constituents at the relevant time points), or data outside the contest's time range (public leaderboard training 2019-01-01 ~ 2023-12-31, public leaderboard validation 2024-01-01 ~ 2024-12-31) to assist training or inference.
* **Unauthorized upload of external data**: Uploading non-platform-provided datasets (including but not limited to alternative data, overseas market data, third-party factor libraries, external pre-trained model weight files) via Notebook for training or inference.
* **Data poisoning**: Manually modifying, fabricating, or injecting numerical values in the score file, rather than generating them end-to-end via the model inference script.

### End-to-End Model Specification Violations

> This section corresponds to the hard constraints in the "Model Specification" chapter of the introduction document. Violation is treated as non-conformance to the track definition.

* **Implicit feature engineering**: Implementing explicit feature engineering—such as cross-field operators, rolling statistics, factor synthesis, or dimensionality reduction—under the guise of model components (custom Layers, loss functions, data loaders); includes but is not limited to calling talib in `Dataset.__getitem__`, or concatenating pre-computed offline factors during forward propagation.
* **Field count overrun**: Model input fields (as defined in the introduction document, time-series length not counted toward field count) exceed 100; or use of fields not provided by the organizer.
* **Lookback window overrun**: The lookback window relied upon by a single inference exceeds 240 trading days.
* **Parameter count out of bounds**: Trainable parameter count below 100,000 or above 100 million; using techniques such as branch freezing or lazy initialization to make the actual training parameter count during retraining exceed the declared range.
* **Use of external pre-trained weights**: Including loading public pre-trained model checkpoints, transferring after pre-training on other datasets, or embedding third-party weight values in code under the guise of "weight initialization."
* **Private leaderboard retraining not reproducible**: The submitted training script cannot reproduce the training process corresponding to the declared weights using the declared random seed and configuration in the platform's isolated environment (including missing dependencies, hardcoded external paths, undeclared sources of randomness, etc.).

### Submission Evasion Violations

* **Cross-sectional coverage evasion**: Deliberately constructing outputs with coverage just above 60% (i.e., missing rate of 40%) but with discriminative scoring power on only a few stocks, to evade validation checks.
* **Multi-model concatenation to bypass correlation constraints**: Splitting a complete model into multiple highly correlated sub-models for separate submission, intending to occupy more ranking slots or "trial-and-error" private leaderboard candidates.
* **Rotational submission to refresh rankings**: Repeatedly submitting non-substantive tweaks to the same model weights (e.g., merely replacing irrelevant layers, slightly altering random seeds), solely to trigger evaluation or refresh the public leaderboard display.
* **Reverse output abuse**: When the model's prediction direction is not significant, simply outputting with a negative sign or renaming as a "new model."

### Identity and Collaboration Violations

* **Multiple-account participation**: A single natural person registering multiple accounts or joining multiple teams.
* **Cross-team code/weight sharing**: Different teams sharing training scripts, model weights, key prompts, or hyperparameters; including distribution under the names of "intra-mentorship division of labor" or "lab sharing."
* **Proxy submission**: Others writing and submitting the work on behalf of a participant, where the participant has no substantive contribution.
* **Collusive score boosting**: Multiple teams conspiring to adjust submission strategies, intending to jointly elevate one party's ranking or suppress competitors.

### Detection Mechanisms

The organizer will conduct violation detection through the following mechanisms; submission is deemed consent:

* **Static code analysis**: Scanning submitted code for suspicious patterns (e.g., time-index shifts, external network requests, implicit feature engineering, suspicious weight loading paths).
* **Output correlation clustering**: Performing correlation matrix analysis on all pooled model scores in the field to identify abnormal clusters.
* **Cross-team fingerprint comparison**: Comparing structural similarity, variable naming, comment style, and model architecture features of code submitted by different teams.
* **Runtime auditing**: Sampling team code for reproduction of training and inference in an isolated environment, comparing model output against the team's claimed logic, parameter count, and input fields for consistency.
* **Private leaderboard retraining audit**: During the private leaderboard phase, when the platform retrains from scratch, automated checks for parameter count statistics, field-usage scanning, and lookback window probing are executed concurrently.

## Rule Versions and Changes

* **Rule version notice**: The current contest rules are a **draft version** and may encounter unavoidable engineering or evaluation issues during the closed beta phase (e.g., data interface changes, evaluation algorithm bottlenecks, third-party service anomalies). **The rule version released "after the closed beta ends and before the official competition begins" shall prevail**; participants are asked to keep an eye on official announcements and community notifications.
* **Rule adjustment rights during the competition**: To safeguard fairness, **the organizer reserves the right to revise rules, adjust evaluation criteria, and supplement anti-cheating mechanisms during the competition**. Rule adjustments will take into account the handling of already-submitted works (e.g., re-scoring, retaining historical scores for reference, providing reasonable code adjustment windows) to minimize the impact on regularly participating teams.
* **Change types and lead time**:

| Change Type | Examples | Lead-Time Notice |
|---|---|---|
| Major changes | Scoring formula modifications, submission specification adjustments, schedule postponement | ≥ 48 hours |
| General changes | Public information field adjustments, local tool version updates | ≥ 24 hours |
| Emergency revisions | Anti-cheating patches, fixes for severe data/platform bugs, security vulnerability response | Immediate notice, with supplementary explanation afterward |

* **Historical version archive**: All rule versions will be retained on the official website with traceable history, allowing participants to verify the rules under which their submissions were made; the version number follows a "major.minor" format, with major changes incrementing the major version number.

## Data and Platform Disclaimer

* **Data usage scope**: Competition data is for **contest evaluation and academic research** only. Onward distribution, commercial use, uploading to public repositories, or use in any scenario outside this competition is strictly prohibited, including after the competition concludes. Once discovered, the organizer will pursue legal liability accordingly.
* **Data confidentiality period**: The confidentiality obligation for sensitive data such as high-frequency snapshots and PIT financial data provided in this contest is **permanently effective** and is not lifted by the conclusion of the competition.
* **Data accuracy**: The organizer has performed necessary cleaning and validation on the data, but does not guarantee absolute accuracy or completeness. Score fluctuations caused by data issues **generally do not constitute grounds for evaluation appeals**, but serious data errors (e.g., bulk missing values, field misalignment, adjustment-factor anomalies) verified after investigation will trigger unified recalculation, with synchronized explanation to all affected teams.
* **Platform stability**: The BigQuant platform is deployed with high availability, but may still experience brief unavailability due to force majeure (data center failures, network outages, third-party dependency anomalies, etc.). For submission delays caused by platform failures, the organizer will reasonably extend the deadline based on the failure duration or cancel the evaluation results from that period; however, **no economic compensation will be provided for participants' compute consumption or time investment**.
* **Compute quota**: GPU Notebook single inference / retraining ≤ 6 hours, public leaderboard phase single inference cap of 12 hours (see "Code Requirements and Platform Constraints" in the introduction document). Run failures caused by exceeding the quota are the participants' responsibility; during the private leaderboard retraining phase, the platform will enforce the above limits, and exceeding them will result in retraining failure for that model.
* **Results do not constitute investment advice**: All models, scores, backtest results, and derivative analyses in this contest are for academic research and contest evaluation only and **do not constitute investment advice of any form**. The organizer, platform, and partners bear no liability for any profits or losses arising from anyone conducting live trading based on them.

## Participant Responsibilities

### Code Compliance and Security

* Participants are responsible for the code they submit, which must not contain:
  * Malicious code, backdoors, or logic for unauthorized access to platform resources;
  * Code attempting to circumvent the platform sandbox, network isolation, or resource limits;
  * Code attempting to obtain other teams' submission contents or intermediate evaluation results;
  * File operations that read from or write to non-sandbox paths in the private leaderboard retraining environment.
* Once discovered, this will be handled as L4 immediately (disqualification), with the right to pursue legal liability reserved.

### Third-Party Dependencies

* If participants use open-source libraries, external APIs, or other third-party resources, they must self-confirm that the license **permits competition and commercial review use cases**.
* **The use of any external pre-trained weights is prohibited**—including but not limited to public HuggingFace checkpoints, torchvision pre-trained models, and transfer-learning weights from one's own projects. All model parameters must be trained from scratch on this competition's data.
* Copyright/license disputes arising from third-party dependencies are borne by the participants; the organizer reserves the right to require teams to replace contested dependencies.

### Personal Information and Likeness

* Personal information provided by participants at registration is used only for contest-related purposes; the organizer will protect it in accordance with the law, but participants must ensure the information provided is true and valid.
* Finalist teams agree that the organizer may use their **names, schools, team names, and finals footage** in contest promotion; if anonymity is to be retained, this must be requested in writing upon finals confirmation.

### Closed Beta and Freeze Period Constraints

* Submissions during the closed beta phase (2026-06-08 to 2026-06-18) do not count toward preliminary scores; the leaderboard resets after the closed beta ends.
* Before the public leaderboard deadline, each team must **designate a unique end-to-end model** from their submission history as their private leaderboard entry (see "Submission Specification and Frequency" in the introduction document); for teams that do not designate one in time, the platform defaults to the team's highest-scoring public-leaderboard submission.
* Once the candidate model is designated, it is frozen. During the private leaderboard phase, participants **may not add to or modify the training script, inference script, dependency declarations, hyperparameter configurations, or random seeds**; the submitted code package must be able to independently complete training and inference in the platform's isolated environment.

## Appeals and Final Interpretation

### Appeal Process

* **Scope of appeals**: Disputes over evaluation results (including public leaderboard score anomalies, private leaderboard retraining failures, style-removal residual anomalies, etc.), objections to violation handling, severe data errors, etc.
* **Appeal channels**: Contact contest operations staff, attaching necessary evidence (screenshots, logs, code snippets, etc.). Appeals through unofficial channels (private contact with judges, community spam, etc.) are **not accepted**.
* **Response timelines**:

| Appeal Type | Initial Response | Final Conclusion |
|---|---|---|
| General appeals (evaluation results, public information) | Within 5 business days | Within 10 business days |
| Objections to violation handling | Within 48 hours | Within 7 business days |
| Emergency appeals (involving deadlines, critical data) | Within 24 hours | Handled together with the emergency revision |

* **Repeat appeals**: For repeat appeals on the same matter, or appeals without new evidence, the organizing committee reserves the right not to respond separately.
